The mobile

A cot mobile in the Calder manner: one large disc and, on a second arm, two smaller ones, each holding the others in balance. Wordmark: round 1 A (outlined Literata 500, joined tt).

Before your child can remember anything, they lie on their back and watch the mobile you hung over the cot. This is that mobile: a big disc and two small ones, each holding the others up, the way a family does. It is the first thing they looked at, and the letters are what they will read when they are older.

The six tests, honestly

1. 2am test
Partial pass
One second: "the cot, the quiet room, someone hung this for my baby." Soft and safe rather than striking. It feels like the nursery, not like a letter.
2. Caption test
Partial pass (1 of 4)
Read cold it says "a baby, a nursery". It says child clearly; it does not say letters, voice or keeping. The name has to carry "letters".
3. Recall test
Pass
"A cot mobile: one big, two small discs." Seven words; parents can draw it in three strokes and three dots.
4. Scale test
Pass at 29 and up, weak at 16
60 and 180 read as a mobile. 29 (small cut) reads as three discs under an arc. 16 is a bar over three dots: a recognisable silhouette, not the idea.
5. Misread test
No death, religious or political reading in the final
Seen in exploration and designed out: gallows (long drop from a level beam; all right angles removed), pram (two discs at one level; now three at three heights), bomb and fuse (fixed disc; now hung on threads). Remaining: a family tree or org chart diagram, a molecule, beamed musical notes, generic baby-mobile clip art.
6. Elite test
Partial pass
The Calder lineage is real and the drawing is quiet, but beside Aesop and Apple Books it reads closer to nursery decor than to a publisher. Craft can raise it; the subject is still a baby object.

Near matches found

Image and web search (Oct 3 2026), not a clearance: no well-known logo uses a cot mobile. Closest: generic baby-mobile clip art and UI icon sets (for example IconPark "baby-mobile"); Flensted Mobiles, a Danish maker of mobiles, sells the object itself; Google's 2011 Calder Doodle. The risk is genericness, not confusion with one owner. A professional search in classes 9, 16 and 42 is still needed.

Sketches and rejected routes

Rejected explorations, one line each (contact sheets: large on paper, reversed on accent, 60/29/16px, 16 and 29 at 8x).

01 six territories
  1 Door left ajar: read as a clock hand or a J; the light only means "door" with a caption.
  2 First curl (kept lock of hair): read as @, e or 6; repeats the round 1 letterform failure.
  3 The grasp (small fist round a finger): read as a match, a toggle or a microphone (banned).
  4 Crib mobile: the one that survived; first pass read as a balance scale.
  5 The lit window: read as a phone, a lock or a keyhole.
  6 The whisper (big cupped form, small dot): read as a moon and planet, which is D1's territory.
02 second pass
  A Grasp v2 with knuckles: a fist or a toy, and a drawn body part.
  B Forehead to forehead: tender but two blobs; says nothing on its own.
  C Lit doorway: a bottle or a gate.
  D Memory jar: clear "kept", but reads as medicine or jam, and Jar is a large Indian savings app.
  E Music box key: scissors, glasses or a nose.
  F Lamp over a cot: Pixar's lamp and ball. Out on sight.
03, 04, 05 pinky promise ("your word, kept"): strongest idea on paper, but without hands the hooks read as a knot, a chain link or the numeral 2. Table lamp: legible but a furniture-shop pictogram. Door from the hall: a fridge.
06, 07 mobile: two pieces on threads read as a pram (banned) or as cherries; the family version (three discs, two levels) reads as a mobile at once.
08 round 1: fixed discs read as a bomb with a fuse.
09 round 2: threads make it a mobile, but big and small at one level is a pram.
10 round 3: long drop from a level beam under a vertical post reads as a gallows. Rejected for the death reading; all right angles removed after this.
11 round 4: the Calder two-piece sweep is the most elegant drawing but reads as a planet and moon or a musical note.
12, 13 rounds 5 and 6: family mobile; chose "small rides highest" (G1) so the composition lifts rather than descends.
14 round 7: tapered wire tips and the heavier small cut; final is H3 with H2's shorter hanger.
