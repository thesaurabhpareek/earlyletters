## Verdict

**Do not submit, and do not let an outside family sign in, on the trust layer as written. The product design is mostly sound; the promises are not.**

M14 turned an on-device app into a server product holding children's names, dates, letters and (with backup) recordings that we can decrypt. The specs absorbed that change. The words around them did not. Today the microphone permission string, the Terms, the Privacy Policy, the health notice, the privacy label and parts of F02, F08 and F17 still tell parents "your recordings stay on your phone" and "we cannot listen", while F08 ships an escrow that lets whoever holds one Edge Function secret and the database open every backed-up recording. The staff-access line promises logging that does not exist for dashboard reads. The live website advertises features and prices that are false today. Counsel is scheduled to review a packet that describes the old product, and the delta list sent to them misses half the documents.

Separately, the California auto-renewal design has three holes the status report cannot fill: purchases by people who never sign in, subscribers who delete their account but keep paying Apple, and subscribers who delete the app and are never seen again (the exact population the law protects).

The sensitive cases are better than most first versions: hide propagation, no loss copy, two-parent cap, no deletion of another person's words. The gaps are in what happens after someone leaves, is removed, or dies, and in whether the founder alone can run those cases safely from day one.

Fix the 8 P0 rows before the counsel delta goes out on Fri 16 Oct (most are text, about two days of work for the S16 and S17 lanes). Fix the P1 rows before the first outside family signs in. I agree with R2-architect on the key model (AR-05) and do not repeat it here; its fix changes the wording in PT-02.
