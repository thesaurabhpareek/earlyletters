## 1. Who she is

Priya-type: first baby, 6 weeks old, 2 a.m. Left arm holds the baby, right thumb is free. iPhone SE 3 (small screen, home button). Sleep deprived: reads slowly, misses small print, makes slips with her thumb. She did not plan to use an app; a friend said "talk, it writes it down". She has never used a baby book app.

## 2. Her night, in her voice

I get the install done with the phone propped on a pillow. First thing it asks is whether I am 18 or older, then I tick a box about terms. Fine, I am too tired to read them and I tick it. The next screen is the book's name and a line that says letters to my child, in my own words and voice. I think: is this for a baby? My baby is six weeks old and cannot read. "Talk for a minute, or type a few lines" calms me. There is a line about my first two letters being free. I am not here for money, I move on.

A screen says "It stays in your words." and shows an example with a crossed-out "um". I like that. I tap Continue without thinking. Then it wants my baby's name. I have a name, so I type it with one thumb. Then "Here yet, or on the way?" I pick Born. The date: I have to scroll a date picker back about six weeks, at 2 a.m., with a baby in my other arm. There is a little "Born today" button, which is no use to me. Then it asks what my baby calls me. She calls me nothing, she is six weeks old. It says "Pick the name you hope to hear." That one I like. I pick Mama.

Then "The book is open." and a card about downloading words, Wi-Fi, a size. I am on Wi-Fi, I tap download. I tap "Write the first one" and land on a home screen with a quiet greeting, "Still up, Mama. Take your time." That is kind. It is also very white and bright. My eyes hurt. There is a question to start me ("Who does she look like?" kind of thing, one of them for the early weeks) and two big buttons, Speak and Type. I tap Speak. It tells me my phone will ask about the microphone. I tap Continue, then iOS asks again. I tap Allow. Now a red circle, no tap needed, it is already listening. I whisper because she is finally asleep. After a few seconds it says "Still here. Take your time." I wonder if it can hear me at all.

She stirs. I tap Pause. I rock her, put the phone face down on the pillow. A minute later I pick it up and the screen is dark. I unlock it and I am looking at a page with my words on it and a line saying the recording stopped when I left the app. I did not leave the app. I wanted to say one more sentence. Now I have to start another recording, and that will be a second letter.

I read my words. There is a small card telling me to have a read, and little marks where it took out an "um". I tap "Add to the book". I did not really understand what "Keep private" would have done. After a moment a card asks about reminders, "evenings", and then my phone asks about notifications. At 2 a.m. I say not now. I put the phone down. Tomorrow morning I will open it and wonder if it is still there. Home will say good morning and offer me a new question. It will not say my letter is safe. I will have to guess that the Book tab is where it went.

## 3. What already works for her (protect it)

Listed so a fix does not break it. All [verified] in the files named.

- Greeting at night is gentle, with no pressure: "Still up, {name}. Take your time." (`strings.en.ts:220`).
- Opening Listen from Speak starts the mic with no second tap, and nothing vibrates or sounds during a take (`listen.tsx:398` comment "No haptic"; keep-awake at `:344`). A sleeping baby is not woken by the app.
- The take is saved before the mic goes live, and every exit keeps what exists (`listen.tsx:1-22` header). "Let it go" is behind a confirm.
- Prompts for the first months are written for her: "What does the middle of the night sound like right now?" (`prompts.ts:34`). No gendered child, no "milestone" pressure.
- Review footer: one 56 pt primary and one quiet choice, pinned at the bottom for a thumb (`review.tsx:871-903`).
- A take with no words is never called empty: "a laugh or a babble" (`words.en.ts` noSpeech comment). Right for a baby in the room.

## 5. Suggested order

1. PER1-001 and PER1-002 together: one device test with a real SE 3, then one decision on Pause and lock. They protect her words, which is the product.
2. PER1-003, PER1-004: two small content and design decisions that change what she understands at the exact moment of choice.
3. PER1-005, PER1-006, PER1-007: the night itself (light, whisper, sleep).
4. The rest are small and can ride along with the first-run work already proposed in NEU-001.

## 6. Where I agree with the existing reviews (no new finding)

- NEU-001, NEU-005: the first-run path is long; for her the date and the "what does she call you" steps are the slowest ones.
- NEU-003 and CDO-A-014: a first letter with no words, and no recovery for a Let it go on a long take.
- NEU-008, NEU-011: two start buttons in a row after first run; the ask arrives on a timer right after the Saved moment.
- NEU-010: Review is heavy; PER1-004 is a narrower point inside it.
- CDO-B-015: Not much today does nothing visible under a store error.
- DSR-001, DSR-024: nothing has been run at accessibility sizes on a phone; PER1-013 and PER1-015 need the same SE 3 run.

## 7. For the founder

- A device test on one iPhone SE 3 (lock, Pause, whisper, brightness, SE fit) would settle PER1-001, 002, 005, 006, 007 and 013 in one sitting. Agents cannot do it.
- PER1-011 and PER1-018 need a product ruling, not a build.
