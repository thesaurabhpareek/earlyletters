## 1. Noor, in her own words

I am thirty-four, I run a team, and in eleven weeks I have a second baby. In two weeks my phone is replaced. I do not put my daughter's voice or her face anywhere I cannot get them back from. I picked this app because the store page says "Never sold, never used for ads" and that is the first claim I check.

The promise screen is good. It shows a fix and lets me put it back, and I trust a thing that shows me its work. I like that the Privacy page says "Anyone who can unlock this iPhone can open the book" instead of "only you". That is the first time an app has not flattered me. The lock is offered once, at the end of first run, and it is off until I say yes.

The money is plain too. The welcome tells me two letters are free and that reading, listening and my copy stay free. I am not surprised by the third letter, because I was told on the first screen. What I do not like is that I recorded a throwaway test letter to see how it behaves, and I have no idea if that was one of my two.

Then the new phone. Where your letters live has a heading "If you get a new phone". Under it: iPhone backup "includes your recordings, if iCloud Backup is on", an export that "is a copy to open and keep", and "Encrypted backup: Not in this version". Nothing says my letters will be on the new phone. I understand why: they have not proven it yet and will not claim it. I respect that, and it still leaves me with a phone swap in two weeks and no answer. The export is a folder I can read, but I cannot see a way to put it back into the app.

Delete is the other test. Delete one letter: it waits 30 days, with "Erase now". Good. "Erase everything on this phone" is a real wipe, clearly worded. But if I ever sign in, that row is gone, and Delete account says my letters stay on this phone. So the day I sign in to move to my new phone is the day I lose the one-tap wipe.

The photo permission I cannot walk at all: there is no primer yet, only a spec. What I read in the spec worries me: the line says it reads "where your pictures were taken". That is my first question, and the second sentence that would answer my second question, whether it looks at what is in them, was removed.

## 2. What already works (do not break)

- The promise is one sentence, used word for word in the store, on Privacy and on the website (`strings.en.ts:1555`; `store.en.ts:76`).
- Privacy says who can see the book as a plain fact while the lock is off (`strings.en.ts:1071-1073`).
- Names in notifications are off by default (`lib/reminders/prefs.ts:44`) and the lock forces them off.
- Export is checkable: a SHA-256 manifest, the original recording byte for byte, steps for a Mac and Windows, and a plain "not locked with a password" warning (`export.en.ts:31`, `:153`, README block).
- Erase everything is clear and honest about what it does not touch: iCloud backups and exports you already saved stay (`strings.en.ts:971`).
- Per-letter delete is gentle and reversible, with an Erase now (`strings.en.ts:891-908`).
- The Start from a photo path uses Apple's picker with no photo permission at all (`lib/photos/picker.ts:3`).
- Sign out keeps letters on the phone and says so (`auth.en.ts:321`).

## 4. The five that matter most

1. PER5-001 and PER5-006: the app does not say whether her book or a held letter reaches a new phone. P1 only because it is honest; lift if the restore drill fails.
2. PER5-003: signing in to move phones takes away the one-tap wipe.
3. PER5-014 and PER5-017: the photo words say what is read or kept and not what is never done; hold the flag until cleared.
4. PER5-004 and PER5-005: "erased for good" and "your free two" both promise more than they hold.
5. PER5-007 and PER5-008: Export leaves pictures out and backup has no delete, both before their flags turn on.
