# Identity design critique: Early Letters symbols A to F, wordmarks W1 to W4

Reviewer stance: senior identity designer, no stake in any option. Benchmarks: Calm, Aesop, Apple Books, Airbnb Belo, Penguin.
Method: every image in the packet viewed (app icon, lockups, home light/dark, sizes incl. 8x magnified, appearances, notification, settings/spotlight, App Store), plus measured glyph bounds and colour contrast on the 1024 masters.

## Verdict up front

None of the six meets the stated bar as delivered. One (B) has a real idea and could reach it after a disciplined refinement pass. A is the safest and most legible but is two stock icons fused together, and it will look generic next to Calm or Apple Books. D, F, E and C are not recommended.

**Ranking: B, A, D, F, E, C.**
**Wordmark: W4** (with a dedicated small cut), W1 as the robust fallback.

## A. Envelope with crescent moon

**Read:** a letter sent at bedtime. Instantly legible.

**Form.** Clean, confident silhouette. Good: the moon and flap are separated by an even offset gap, and the flap's V has a softened inner vertex. Problems: (1) Corner radii don't match. The envelope's bottom corners are about 50 px, while the flap tips and top corners are about 15 px, so the base looks like a different drawing from the top. (2) The moon's terminals don't match either. The upper horn ends in a blunt flat cut and the lower horn tapers to a point. (3) The mass centre sits 100 px below the box centre. The heavy envelope base drags the whole glyph down, so it reads low in the tile at every size.

**Simplicity vs clip-art.** High clip-art risk. Envelope plus moon is the "Mail + Sleep" SF Symbols combination. On the home-screen crops it sits directly above a moon-icon sleep app and beside Books, and blends into utility-icon territory.

**Ownability.** Low. Many "night mode mail" and newsletter apps use this. It is hard to protect and hard to make iconic.

**Wordmark pairing.** Rounded geometry fights the bookish serif. It looks like a productivity tool next to a literary name.

**Small sizes.** The best of the six. Still clear at 29 px. At 16 px the moon merges with the flap gap, but it stays a recognisable blob.

**Premium signal.** Medium-low. Tidy, but reads "utility".

| simple | elegant | communicative | expressive | elite | craft | recall | small-size |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 6 | 8 | 5 | 5 | 7 | 6 | 8 |

**For:** the most legible concept in the set. It tells the story (letter + bedtime) in under a second.
**Against:** a combination of two system icons is not a brand. It will never feel elite or ownable.

**Fixes.**
- Raise the whole glyph by about 40 px so the optical centre sits slightly above the tile centre.
- Unify radii: one radius (about 24 px at 1024) for every corner, including the flap tips.
- Give both moon horns the same terminal: both tapered, or both cut on the same angle.
- Shrink the moon by 10 percent and tuck it so it rises out of the envelope (a letter emerging), rather than resting on top of it as a separate pictogram. That makes one idea instead of two icons.
- Consider dropping the flap V entirely: an open rectangle with the moon rising from it is more ownable.

## B. Two quotation marks, large and small (parent and child)

**Read:** an opening quote, "the words, exactly as said". The large mark curls protectively over the small one, which reads as parent and child. This is the only candidate that carries the tagline, the voice, and the relationship at the same time.

**Form.** Expressive and calligraphic, with real character. Craft is unfinished:
1. **The large mark has a hard notch at about (470, 400-430)**, where the tail joins the bowl. There is no tangent continuity, and it reads as a boolean seam.
2. The small mark has the same kind of notch where its stem meets the bowl (about 755, 545).
3. **The two marks come from different construction logics.** The large bowl is a near-perfect circle with a long swan-neck tail. The small bowl is an egg with a short upright stem. The story ("the child is a small version of the parent") is stronger if the small mark is literally the same drawing, scaled.
4. The terminals don't match: a blunt angled cap on the large tail, a squared nub on the small one.
5. The large tail runs to x=838 and overhangs top right, so the glyph leans right and the top-right corner of the tile feels crowded.

**Simplicity vs clip-art.** Low clip-art risk. It is a drawn mark, not a pictogram. Mis-read risks worth naming honestly: "66", cherries, musical notes, and (teardrop + tail) tadpoles or sperm. Fixing the joins and shortening the tail reduces the tadpole reading considerably.

**Ownability.** High *if* refined. Quotation marks as a generic idea are common (quote apps, review sites). The scaled pair with the sheltering tail is distinctive and protectable. Its nearest benchmark is Airbnb Belo, which also has a single gestural stroke and an emotional reading.

**Wordmark pairing.** Best in the set, especially with W4. The marks are typographic in origin, so symbol and name come from one family.

**Colour.** Paper on sepia looks warm and confident here. The large white mass reads as a bold, poster-like heirloom, not muddy.

**Small sizes.** Good down to 29 px. At 16 px the gap between the marks closes and the tail thins to a 1 px wisp. A small optical master is needed: thicker tail, wider gap.

| simple | elegant | communicative | expressive | elite | craft | recall | small-size |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | 8 | 8 | 9 | 8 | 6 | 9 | 7 |

**For:** the only mark that is an *idea* rather than an object. It encodes "exactly as you said it" and "I was loved before I can remember" (the large sheltering the small). It is memorable after one look.
**Against:** the drawing is not finished. The joins, terminals and mismatched construction would be caught in the first review at any top studio. Teardrop shapes with tails also carry a real (if fixable) tadpole reading.

**Fixes (in order).**
1. Redraw the small mark as an exact 55 to 60 percent scale of the large mark, the same drawing, so they read as one family.
2. Make the bowl-to-tail joins continuous (G2 curvature). No inside notches.
3. Shorten the large tail by about 8 percent and lower its apex by about 30 px. End it at the small mark's vertical axis, so it shelters the child without overhanging the tile corner.
4. Give every terminal one cut: angled flat at the same angle, or a soft ball. Not both.
5. Re-centre: the box currently sits 13 px right. Shift left so the combined mass centre is on x=512, and nudge up about 20 px.
6. Ship a small optical master for 16 to 40 px: tail stroke at least 1/18 of glyph height, and inter-mark gap widened 15 percent.
7. Check closing-quote readings in RTL contexts (Arabic is a target language). Arabic uses « » guillemets, so the mark reads as a shape there, not punctuation. Acceptable, but test it.

## C. Open book with a tilted card

**Read:** ambiguous. A book? A "W" or "M"? Mountains with a falling sign? A ballot box? The tilted rounded square does not read as a letter. It reads as a tile, a tooth, or an ice cube.

**Form.** Page tops end in blobby rounded knobs that conflict with the sharp outer page corners. The gutter is a hairline that vanishes at 29 px. The glyph is only 33 percent of the tile height, so it looks undersized and floats. The card's rotation (about 30 degrees) has no relationship to any other angle in the mark.

**Clip-art risk:** high (book). **Ownability:** low. Apple Books sits on the same home screen with an open-book icon.

| simple | elegant | communicative | expressive | elite | craft | recall | small-size |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 4 | 5 | 5 | 3 | 4 | 4 | 4 |

**For:** "memory book" is literally depicted.
**Against:** incoherent at a glance, collides with Apple Books, and the weakest craft in the set.
**Fixes if pursued (not recommended):** scale up to 50 percent height, square off or fully round the page tops consistently, align the card's angle to the page slope, widen the gutter to at least 3 percent of the tile. Honestly, retire it.

## D. Nested eccentric circles tangent at the base

**Read:** generations, nesting, ripples, a womb. Also an eye, a target, an onion, a snail shell. Nothing about letters, voice or words.

**Form.** Strong geometric idea with good contrast modulation (each ring thick on one side, thin on the other). Problems: the tangency points at the bottom pinch into slivers. At 1024 there is a near-zero-width wedge around (470-560, 775), and at 29 to 40 px the rings fuse into moire (see the 8x magnified row). Ring weights are not on a clear progression, so the inner rings look arbitrary next to the outer. On paper, the dark version reads like a staring eye.

**Ownability:** medium. The geometry is clean but generic (wellness, fintech and audio brands use concentric rings).

| simple | elegant | communicative | expressive | elite | craft | recall | small-size |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | 7 | 3 | 6 | 6 | 5 | 6 | 4 |

**For:** the most "elite-looking" abstract geometry, with a quiet generational metaphor.
**Against:** communicates nothing about the product. The founder already rejected a mark for "communicating nothing", and this repeats that failure in a more beautiful form. It also breaks down at small sizes.
**Fixes:** reduce to three elements (outer ring, one inner ring, dot), separate tangencies by a minimum 2.5 percent gap, and put ring weights on a ratio (for example 1 : 0.62 : 0.38).

## E. Folded card with a heart cut-out

**Read:** greeting card, Valentine, Hallmark. Also reads as an open door.

**Form.** The heart is split by the fold, and its two lobes are visibly different sizes and curvatures. The left lobe is fuller, the right one is compressed by the perspective panel. That reads as lopsided, not as perspective. The right panel's trapezoid perspective is mild and inconsistent with the flat left panel. Corner radii are small and uniform (good), but the tall slab is the heaviest mass in the set (24 percent ink) and looks blocky on the home screen.

**Clip-art risk:** very high (heart). **Elite:** low. A heart is the most mass-market emotional signifier there is, and it pushes the brand towards gift-shop.

| simple | elegant | communicative | expressive | elite | craft | recall | small-size |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | 5 | 7 | 5 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |

**For:** warm and instantly emotional. It holds up at small sizes.
**Against:** cliché, cheap-feeling, and it undermines "grown-up, heirloom, never babyish".
**Fixes if pursued:** remove the heart and let the opening card carry the meaning (for example a small sound-wave or quote shape inside). Make the heart symmetric if it stays. Not recommended.

## F. Nursery mobile (Calder-like)

**Read:** baby nursery, a Calder mobile. Grown-up through the art reference, but it says "baby product", not "letters" or "voice".

**Form.** Elegant at 180 px and above. Craft problems: the arms are uneven in weight, with lumpy bulges at the joints (about 240,430 and 680,400) that look unintentional. The hanging hook is a separate, thinner stroke. Wires go below 1 px at 40 px and below, so the mark falls apart into three floating dots (see the Settings and notification crops). Mass centre x=425: the composition hangs left and low, and the 74 percent width pushes the large circle toward the squircle edge. Only 8 percent ink, so it looks faint on the home screen.

**Ownability:** low to medium. Mobiles are common in baby and nursery branding.

| simple | elegant | communicative | expressive | elite | craft | recall | small-size |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | 7 | 5 | 7 | 6 | 4 | 6 | 3 |

**For:** genuinely charming, art-literate, and balanced in concept (big, medium and small, like a family).
**Against:** illegible below 60 px, communicates the nursery rather than the product, and the line work is unrefined.
**Fixes:** stroke at least 1/22 of tile width, uniform and monoline. Clean mitred or rounded joints. Rebalance the arms so the mass centre sits on x=512. Shrink to 66 percent width. Ship a small master with no hook and thicker arms.

## Colour (all candidates use the same treatment)

- **Sepia #8A5A3B tile with a paper glyph** is clean, not muddy, at 5.5:1. But as a flat fill it reads "chocolate / coffee / parcel brown" more than "leather-bound heirloom", especially beside saturated system icons on a blue wallpaper.
- Refinements within the brand family:
  1. Add a very subtle vertical gradient, about #94603F at the top to #7C4F33 at the bottom. This gives depth like Apple Books or Calm without gimmick, and works with iOS 26's glass.
  2. Optionally nudge the tile a few degrees warmer and redder (about #8E5636) so it reads as terracotta or leather rather than coffee.
  3. Keep the glyph at paper #FBF8F3, not pure white. That is correct and premium.
  4. Dark appearance (#D9A47E on a near-black tile) is the best-looking state in the whole packet. Keep it.
  5. Explore one alternate for marketing: a paper tile with a sepia glyph. It suits stationery and heirloom (Aesop-like), but has lower shelf contrast on light wallpapers, so use it only for print and web, not the app icon.
- Never set sepia text on ink (2.6:1).

## Wordmarks

| | Character | 28 px | 14 px | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **W1** | Sturdy transitional text serif (the lockup face) | Clear, even | Most legible of the four | Competent but generic. Reads like a well-set app UI, not a brand. Its small cut is pixel-identical to W2's small cut. |
| **W2** | High-contrast display serif | Elegant | **Hairlines break up** at 14 px; "a", "e" and "s" lose their thins | Its small cut is the same as W1's small cut, so it is not a distinct system: W2 is "W1 with a display master". |
| **W3** | Letterspaced small caps | Formal and classical | Readable | Reads as wedding stationery or a law firm. At the same cap height it is **2.3x wider** than W4 (339 px vs 145 px), which hurts lockups, nav bars and the App Store title. It is also cold, not intimate. |
| **W4** | Old-style Garamond-type, "tt" ligature | Warmest, most literary | Small x-height. Counters in "e" and "a" start to close, and the word space between "Early" and "Letters" nearly vanishes | Closest to Penguin, Aesop and heirloom books. Its humanist stress pairs best with the calligraphic B mark. |

**Pick: W4.** It is the only one with an emotional voice: "a letter from someone who loves you", set in a book face. Conditions:
- Use W4-master from 20 px upward. Below that, use W4-small with +2 to 3 percent tracking.
- Open the word space by about 15 percent at all sizes. "EarlyLetters" currently runs together at 14 px.
- Check the "y"-to-"L" kern so the descender of "y" does not crowd the "L".
- Keep W1 as the UI and system fallback for anywhere under 13 px or where rendering is poor (Android, email clients).
- Drop W2 (redundant with W1 and fragile when small) and W3 (too wide and too formal).

## Top insights

1. **Only B is an idea, and it is unfinished.** The two quotation marks (large sheltering small) are the one mark that carries the tagline, the voice, and the parent-child bond together. Its execution has seam-like notches, mismatched terminals, and two drawings where there should be one. A proper refinement pass (scaled twin, G2 joins, shorter tail, small optical master) is the highest-value next step.
2. **The others depict objects; they don't express meaning.** Envelope, book, card, mobile, rings: each is either a stock pictogram (A, C, E), off-message (F), or abstract to the point of saying nothing (D, the same failure as the rejected "e"). A is the fallback if B can't be resolved, but it will stay generic.
3. **The system is stronger than any single mark.** Sepia and paper is good but flat, and a subtle gradient lifts it. The dark appearance is the best state in the packet. W4 gives the brand its literary warmth. Pair B with W4, refine both, and test at 29 px before anything else.
