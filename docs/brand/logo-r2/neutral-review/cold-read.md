# Cold read: logo options A to F

## Step 1: one-second impressions (written before reading PRODUCT.md, unedited)

All six share the same brown tile and cream glyph, so colour does no differentiating work; it reads "warm, craft, coffee/leather". On the home screen the tile is noticeably darker and duller than its neighbours.

**A (envelope + crescent moon)**
- See: an open envelope with a crescent moon rising out of it.
- Guess: a sleep / bedtime-story app, or a "night mail" / newsletter app.
- Feeling: calm, quiet, bedtime.
- Awkward: the moon dominates; it reads "sleep" before "letter". On the mock home screen it sits two rows above an actual moon icon (teal sleep-style app) and looks related to it. Moon slightly overlaps envelope flap, so the top edge of the envelope is lost; envelope reads as a bowl/cup holding a moon.

**B (two comma / quote shapes, big and small)**
- See: a big and a small teardrop with hooked tails. Opening quotation mark "66", or two commas, or music notes, or cherries.
- Guess: quotes / reading / podcast / a fruit brand.
- Feeling: organic, soft, a bit playful; parent-and-child pairing comes through vaguely.
- Awkward: honestly, two swimming teardrops with tails can read as tadpoles or sperm at a glance. That is a real risk for a baby/family product. The long hairline tail on the big shape also looks fragile.

**C (open book + tilted rectangle)**
- See: an open book whose pages curve up like wings, with a small tilted tile hovering above the gutter.
- Guess: education / reading / library app; maybe a ballot or "drop something in" app.
- Feeling: neutral, slightly confusing.
- Awkward: the floating rectangle is unexplained (sugar cube? stamp? card? tooth?). Silhouette also reads as an "M" or a pair of cupped hands or a valley. On the home screen it sits directly above Apple Books' open-book icon, which makes it look like a derivative.

**D (nested circles, offset to bottom)**
- See: three/four nested circles all touching at the bottom: an eye, a lens, a snail shell, ripples, a target, matryoshka.
- Guess: meditation, camera, focus or audio app.
- Feeling: calm, abstract, generic-premium.
- Awkward: reads as an eyeball or a camera lens first. Nothing about letters, voice or babies. Could be any wellness startup.

**E (folded card with heart cutout)**
- See: a folded greeting card, slightly open, with a heart cut out across the fold. Also a door ajar.
- Guess: greeting-card / e-card app, Valentine's, dating.
- Feeling: sweet, clear, a bit stock.
- Awkward: heart-on-card is a very common clip-art move; feels like Hallmark/Paperless Post. Heart also evokes playing cards.

**F (crib mobile)**
- See: a hanging nursery mobile with three balls of decreasing size.
- Guess: a baby / nursery / parenting app. Immediately.
- Feeling: tender, gentle, Calder-ish, modern-nursery.
- Awkward: thin wires will vanish at small sizes. Could read as a molecule or a network diagram. Says "baby" but not "letters" or "voice". Composition is lopsided (heavy left ball, mark leans right).

## Step 2: scored critique (after PRODUCT.md and all remaining images)

Brief recap of the bar: voice letters from parents to a baby, read years later. "Exactly as you said it." "I was loved before I can remember." Tender, heirloom, grown-up, never babyish. Founder wants simple, elegant, communicative, expressive, elite.

### Scores (1 to 10)

| | Simple | Elegant | Communicative | Expressive | Elite | Recall | Small size (16/29) | Total /70 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A envelope + moon | 8 | 6 | 6 | 5 | 5 | 8 | 7 | 45 |
| B two quote drops | 8 | 8 | 5 | 7 | 8 | 7 | 7 | **50** |
| C book + tile | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 5 | 29 |
| D nested circles | 8 | 7 | 2 | 5 | 7 | 7 | 4 | 40 |
| E card + heart | 9 | 6 | 7 | 6 | 4 | 9 | 8 | 49 |
| F mobile | 6 | 7 | 6 | 8 | 5 | 7 | 3 | 42 |

"Communicative" here means: would a stranger guess "letters / words to a child"? None scores above 7, because no option carries both halves of the product (written/spoken words AND a child/love).

### A. Envelope + crescent moon
- For: the only mark that literally says "letter" and "bedtime" (the moment parents record). Very drawable, survives 16px.
- Against: the moon is the bigger, brighter shape, so it reads "sleep app" first; envelope-plus-icon is a stock clip-art construction, not elite. On the home-screen mock it rhymes with an actual moon icon two rows down.
- Fixes: demote the moon (small crescent as a wax seal on the flap, or cut the flap edge into a crescent) so the envelope leads; give the envelope a closed or half-open flap so its silhouette is complete (currently the moon eats the top edge and it reads as a bowl).

### B. Two quotation-drops, big and small
- For: the most ownable, crafted, premium silhouette of the six; a quotation mark ties straight to "Exactly as you said it", and the big/small pair quietly reads parent and child. Holds up on dark, tinted and clear appearances.
- Against: at first glance it is not obviously a quotation mark; it reads as two tadpoles/commas, and the swimming-teardrop-with-tail reading (sperm) is a genuine risk for a baby product. The large shape's long hairline hook is fragile and is what drives that reading.
- Fixes: redraw as an unmistakable typographic opening quote ("66") with short, thick tails and matching construction for both glyphs (today they are two different shapes: a big hooked comma and a small onion-drop); let the small one lean into the big one for tenderness; run a 10-stranger 1-second test specifically for "tadpole/sperm". If that read persists after redraw, drop the route.

### C. Open book + tilted tile
- For: "book" is on-category (memory book) and the page curves have some warmth.
- Against: the floating tilted rectangle has no legible meaning, and the whole reads as an "M", cupped hands or a valley. Sits next to Apple Books' open-book icon and looks derivative.
- Fixes: I would not iterate this one. If the book idea matters, the object rising from the gutter needs to be instantly readable (a folded note, a sound wave), and the silhouette must not be a plain open book.

### D. Nested circles
- For: calm, balanced, premium-abstract; nice idea of generations / a child held inside.
- Against: communicates nothing about the product; reads as an eye, lens, snail or target, i.e. any wellness or camera app. The thin ring gaps collapse into grey mush at 16 and 29px.
- Fixes: if kept, reduce to two shapes (one large, one small, touching), thicken gaps, and add one cue of voice or paper. In practice this is the "letterform e" problem again: abstract and elegant, communicates nothing, which the founder already rejected.

### E. Folded card with heart cutout
- For: clearest at every size (best 16px of the six) and the fastest "love + card/letter" read; easiest to draw from memory.
- Against: heart-on-greeting-card is the most generic construction here; it reads Hallmark / Valentine / e-card, not heirloom, and nothing about voice or a child. Elite score suffers most.
- Fixes: remove the literal heart; let the negative space of the fold or a slightly opened flap do the emotional work (e.g. the gap between the two leaves shaped like a small figure, or a quote mark). Proportion it as a letter or note card rather than a door.

### F. Hanging mobile
- For: the most tender and instantly "baby" of the six; Calder-like, warm, would make a lovely secondary illustration.
- Against: says "nursery" not "letters", and the brief says never babyish; the hairline wires disappear below 40px (at 16/29 it's three dots, reads like "ooo" or a molecule). The arc strokes also look uneven in weight, which undercuts "premium".
- Fixes: thicken wires to at least ~1/3 ball diameter, clean up the stroke weights, and consider replacing one ball with a small envelope or quote so it tells the product. Better used as brand illustration than as the app icon.

### Cross-cutting notes
- The sepia tile is identical across all six, so colour gives no differentiation and on the home screen it reads dull/muddy next to saturated system icons. A paper (#FBF8F3) tile with a sepia mark would read more like stationery and heirloom, and would stand out more on most wallpapers. Worth testing for whichever symbol wins.
- Home-screen neighbours matter: A collides with sleep/moon apps, C with Books.
- Wordmarks: W2 (high-contrast display serif) reads most premium/heirloom; W1 is sturdy and clear but plainer; W3 (spaced small caps) is formal stationery and could feel stiff; W4's tight spacing makes "EarlyLetters" nearly run together at 14px.

## Ranking
1. **B** (most elite and ownable; concept matches tagline; needs a redraw to kill the tadpole read)
2. **E** (most communicative and legible; too generic to be elite)
3. **A** (says letters + bedtime; moon over-dominates)
4. **F** (most tender; babyish and fails small sizes)
5. **D** (elegant, communicates nothing)
6. **C** (confusing)

## Does any meet the bar?
Plainly, no. None is simultaneously communicative and elite. B is the closest on craft and ownability; E is the closest on clarity. Each option carries only half the story: words/letters (A, B, E) or child/love (D, E, F).

Direction that would: a single, unmistakable **quotation mark built as a big-and-small pair** (B's idea, redrawn to read as typography first), or **a letter/envelope whose flap or fold is that quotation mark**. That unites "letters", "exactly as you said it" and "parent and child" in one silhouette, stays simple enough for 16px, and avoids hearts, moons and nursery tropes. Test on a paper-coloured tile as well as sepia.
