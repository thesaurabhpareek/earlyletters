# Round 2: brand strategy critique of finalists X and Y (both redraws of B)

Role: brand strategist, neutral. I used only the blind2 packet: the overview and every image in X/ and Y/, including the 16px and 29px small cuts. I did not look at the repo or at any rationale.

## The two drawings
- **X** is a soft, round pair. Each mark is a teardrop whose tail curls back over itself into a blunt, rounded knob, like a "6". The small mark is about 65 to 70% the height of the large one, and the pair is set close together. It reads as warm, tactile and spoken.
- **Y** is a sharper pair. The bowls are rounder and the tails taper to pointed, angled hooks, much like a Garamond or Caslon opening quote set at two sizes. It reads as editorial, literary and more formal.

**Implication:** the quote mark itself is crowded, with at least 9 quote apps found, most in the motivational-quotes genre. That genre is a premium-signal hazard: on a small screen the icon can be mistaken for a daily-quotes app. **Ownability lives entirely in two things: the size difference and the specific drawing of the stroke.** Whichever finalist exaggerates its own drawing more wins.

## Judgement

**Pick: X.** In this landscape Y is the more refined typography but the more generic mark: it looks like a quote someone set in a font. X looks like something someone drew, which is what makes it ownable and memorable. X is also warmer and closer to "tender, intimate" and to a voice speaking. The two are close on craft; X wins on strategy.

## Must-fix for X
1. **Add tension to the tail terminals.** The blunt knobs drift toward cartoon, tadpole or a "6 6" reading. Taper or cut the ends slightly so the mark reads as hand-lettered heirloom rather than babyish, while keeping the curl.
2. **Lock the size ratio and spacing as a brand rule.** The size difference is the only ownable idea, so never let anyone set the pair at equal size.
3. **Keep the dedicated small-size cut** (it works) and specify it for 16 to 40px.
4. **Separate the brand from the motivational-quote genre** with colour (sepia and paper, never black and yellow), the wordmark lockup and in-app use. The marks should frame real family words, not slogans.
5. **Before filing:** run the USPTO design-code search and file the specific drawing as a design mark, not "quotation marks".

## Verdict on the founder's bar
**Conditional yes for X.** It is simple, communicative (tagline and parent-child), expressive and highly memorable, and it is the only option in either round that is both relatable and distinctive in its landscape. The "elite" bar is met only once the tail terminals are refined (must-fix 1). As drawn today, it is about 90% there. **Y does not meet the bar**, because its elegance comes from a generic glyph.
