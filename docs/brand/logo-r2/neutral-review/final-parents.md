# Round 2, parent empathy: finalists X and Y (redrawn quotation-mark pair)

**Caveat:** this is simulated judgement by one model, not research with parents.

What I looked at, for both X and Y:
- overview and 1024px icon
- home screen in light, dark and tinted
- appearances
- notification
- settings and spotlight
- sizes sheet
- 16px and 29px cuts, magnified 12x
- lockups

- **X:** soft, plump, rounded marks. The tails end in soft curled hooks, and the small mark sits close to the big one.
- **Y:** crisper, classic book-face quotation marks. The tails are cut straight and slanted, and the gap between the marks is wider.

## Did the tadpole/sperm read go away?

**Mostly yes, in both.** The long trailing whip-tails are gone. Both now read first as a pair of opening quotation marks, big and small.

What remains:
- **X:** the plump bulbs can read as a numeral **"66"** or as two droplets. A few people may see a teardrop, but it is mild because the bulbs are round, not pointed.
- **Y:** no biological reading. Its new risk is that it looks **generic**: this exact glyph is the standard "quote" icon in blogging, notes and quote-of-the-day apps.

## Through the six parents

| | X (soft) | Y (crisp) |
|---|---|---|
| **New mum at 2am, dark mode** | Soft and cuddly. "Something gentle." Feels like two figures leaning together. | Neat and editorial. "A reading or writing app." Cooler. |
| **New dad** | "Quotes? Ok, the big one and the little one, I get it." | "Quotes." The parent-and-child idea is less felt, because the gap is wider and the forms are more formal. |
| **Same-sex couple** | Neutral and inclusive. Two marks, no family-size or gender implication. | Same. |
| **Nani (Hindi speaker)** | "66?" Pleasant shape. Understands it once told it is "words, exactly". | "Quote marks, like in newspaper." Recognised, but cold. |
| **Abuela (Spanish speaker)** | "Comillas... o un 66." Warm reaction to the roundness. | "Comillas." Recognised, with no warmth. |
| **Multilingual family** | Curly quotes are used in English and Chinese. Arabic and French families use « », so for them this is a borrowed sign, though still widely recognised. Same for both marks. | Same. |

- **"For my child":** better than round 1 in both, carried by the big-and-small pair. It is felt more in X, because the small mark nestles into the big one. Neither says *child* or *letters* without the name underneath.
- **Gift pride:** both are high. Y looks more "luxury stationery". X looks more tender and heirloom.
- **Upsetting reads:** nothing serious in either. X has a mild teardrop possibility. Y's slanted cut tails look slightly claw- or hook-like at small sizes.

## Scores (1 to 10)

| | simple | elegant | communicative | expressive | elite | empathy | recall | small size |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **X** | 9 | 8 | 5 | 8 | 7 | 8 | 8 | 8 |
| **Y** | 9 | 8 | 5 | 6 | 8 | 6 | 6 | 6 |

- **Small size:** X's fat forms hold at 29px and mostly at 16px. Y's thin tails break into separate specks at 16px.
- **Recall:** Y is the common quote glyph, which hurts its recall. X's silhouette is more ownable.

## Pick: X

X does what the audience needs: it feels like a big one holding a little one before anyone decodes it as quotation marks. It survives small sizes and dark mode, and it is more ownable. Y is the more refined typographic object, but it reads as "a quote app", and that loses the parent and child.

## Must-fix for X

1. **Kill the "66" read.** Make the small mark lean in a few degrees, and tuck its top hook just under the big bulb with a hairline gap, so it reads as a held pair rather than two digits on a baseline. Do not let them touch, or it becomes one blob at 29px.
2. **Make the tails unmistakably quote-hooks.** Keep them short and curled back toward the bulb (as now), and slightly shorten the big mark's hook. It currently overhangs right, which pushes it toward a "6".
3. **Ship a symbol-small for 16 to 29px.** Thicken the hooks by about 15%, or simplify them, so the hook does not detach into a speck (it is visible at 16px now).
4. **Teardrop check.** Keep the bulbs fully round at the bottom and never sharpen the top. Recheck in tinted-light, where the mark reads darker and more drop-like.
5. **Let the words carry "for my child".** The icon alone will not. The App Store subtitle and first screen should do it, for example "Letters to your child, in your voice".

If Y is chosen instead: soften the cut tail terminals, thicken the tails, and close the gap between the marks. Each of these moves it toward X.

## Verdict on the founder's bar

**Not fully. Yes on five of six.**
- X meets simple, elegant, expressive, empathetic and recall, and is close on elite.
- It does not meet "communicative" on its own: it says *words, exactly as said, big and small*, not *letters to my child*.

That is normal for strong app icons, where the name and subtitle do the explaining. Whether it is acceptable is the founder's call, and it should be tested rather than assumed.

## Cheap test (about $100 to $200)

Run a 5-second test on Lyssna or Maze:
- **Who:** about 60 US parents of children under 3, plus about 20 grandparents, including Spanish- and Hindi-speaking respondents.
- **What to show:** X, Y and one literal control (round 1's A or E), each in a home-screen grid, light and dark, and at 29px.
- **Ask:** "What does this app do?" and "How does it make you feel?"
- **Code the answers for:** quote or words, parent and child, "66", tadpole or sperm, teardrop, and "generic quote app".
- **Then:** reveal the name and tagline and ask "Which would you be proud to gift?"
