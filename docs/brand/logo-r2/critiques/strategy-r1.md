### Colour map
- Baby memory apps: coral, red, teal, lime, purple, pastel gradients. Loud, cheerful, saturated.
- Sleep apps: navy, indigo, purple, with yellow-white moons.
- Grown-up family-story brands: deep green (Storyworth, Remento), navy (Hereafter, Forevermore).
- Premium benchmarks: Day One sky blue, Calm blue gradient, Apple Books orange, Headspace orange; Aesop has no symbol at all (wordmark only, brown and cream packaging).
- **Open space: warm sepia brown with paper cream.** Only one minor app (Moments: Family Memories) sits there. Our `#8A5A3B` field is the most distinctive thing we already own. A light paper icon would sit among Letterbox, Dearest, Keepy and Short Years (all cream): the sepia field is the stronger shelf position. I would keep sepia as the primary icon field unless a concept proves otherwise.

### What is unclaimed (the white space)
1. **A mark about the relationship, not the object.** Every competitor draws a thing (envelope, book, sprout, moon, camera). Nobody draws *from one person to one small person*. Size difference (big and small), direction (toward), and holding are unclaimed.
2. **A single-stroke, drawable symbol at the premium end.** Day One's bookmark is the only true recall mark in the journal space, and it is not about children or family. A parent should be able to draw ours on a birthday card.
3. **Warm, still, unhurried.** The category shouts (gradients, glitter, mascots, wax seals). Stillness reads as premium here by contrast.
4. **Time / years, without a clock or a calendar.** Nobody signals "read years later" visually.

### The traps, ranked
1. Envelope (crowded and death-adjacent via Hereafter; romance via Unsent and Love Letters).
2. Crescent moon (sleep shelf).
3. Monogram (Dearest, Qeepsake, Storyworth; no feeling).
4. Open book or bookmark (Apple Books, Day One).
5. Heart (every love-letter app). The brief already restricts hearts to emergent negative space; even then, a heart is the most common element on the letters shelf.

### Name fit: what "Early Letters" asks of a mark
"Early" = the beginning of a life, and the earliness of the reader (before they can remember). "Letters" = words addressed to someone. The name already says *letters*, so a mark that also draws a letter (envelope, page) is redundant: it repeats the name instead of adding the half the name does not carry (*from whom*, *to whom*, *kept for years*, *spoken*). Strategically the best mark pairs with the name, it does not illustrate it. Penguin draws a penguin; Apple Books draws a book; but Day One draws a bookmark (keeping your place over time) and Airbnb's Bélo draws belonging, not a house. That is the bar.

### How I will judge (strategy lens, each concept)
- **Distinctiveness** against the clusters above (1 to 10).
- **Recall**: can it be drawn from memory in ~5 seconds; under-8-word description.
- **Name fit**: does it add to "Early Letters" rather than repeat it.
- **System potential**: pattern, motion (app launch, saving a letter), cloth book emboss, gift card, invite card (A6 envelope), favicon.
- **Premium signal**: next to Aesop, Calm, Apple Books, Day One.
- Mapped onto the brief's six tests where strategy has a view (2am, caption, recall, misread, elite); scale left mainly to production and craft critics, but I note silhouette failures I can see.
