## 0. Summary for the founder

**Top risks (ranked by likelihood x impact):**

1. **Deceptive privacy claims (FTC Act s.5).** Shipped copy already says things the architecture does not do. "Without backup, everything stays only on this phone" is false once a user signs in, because letter text syncs to Supabase (ARCH s.4 step 7; PRD C s.4.1). "Transcription happens on your phone" is false when the user consents to server fallback (ADR 0002). "Encrypted" backup is decryptable by us in the default escrow mode (ADR 0006). Full audit in s.3. Fix before any public page or store listing goes live.
2. **Server-side mental-health inferences.** `packages/core/src/safety.ts` tiers entries for suicidal ideation and thoughts of harming the baby, and the `safety_events` table stores `tier` with `author_id` on the server. A tier-2 row is an identifiable mental-health inference: sensitive personal information (CCPA), sensitive data (Connecticut and others), consumer health data (Washington MHMDA, which expressly covers inferences). Keep tiers on the device (LEGAL-REQ-015).
3. **Sensitive-data consent now required in Connecticut regardless of size.** From 1 July 2026 the CTDPA applies to any controller that processes sensitive data, and sensitive data needs opt-in consent [L19][L20]. Parents will write about a child's illness, their own postpartum mood, and so on. Combined with MHMDA (no size threshold, private right of action) this means a separate, plain-language sensitive-data consent at account creation (LEGAL-REQ-006) and a separate Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy.
4. **California Automatic Renewal Law timing.** PRD C-REQ-026 sends the annual renewal notice 7 days before. The ARL requires 15 to 45 days for terms of one year or longer, an annual reminder for every subscription, and price-change notice 7 to 30 days ahead [L7]. Easy fix, but a common class-action target.
5. **Usage analytics before consent.** Apple 5.1.1(ii) requires consent for usage data "even if such data is considered to be anonymous" [L13]. PRD A s.7 proposes analytics on by default in the US and A-NFR-002 starts analytics after first frame. Make analytics opt-in (LEGAL-REQ-003).
6. **Age-assurance laws reached app developers in 2026.** Texas SB 2420 is in effect after the Supreme Court declined to block it on 6 July 2026; Apple requires Declared Age Range, Significant Change and parental-consent handling for Texas users since 4 June 2026 [L14][L15]. California's Digital Age Assurance Act follows on 1 January 2027 (Secondary). We need an 18+ gate that also consumes store age signals.
7. **Child-directed signals.** The store keyword list includes "kids" and the description says "let them listen on their own" (`packages/content/src/store.en.ts` lines 11 and 23). Those are COPPA "directed to children" factors [L1 FAQ D.1], conflict with Apple 2.3.8 [L13], and invite Google Play to reclassify our target audience [L17]. Remove. Sibling letters (B-REQ-020) and teen web contributors are the only paths by which we would collect from a child; gate them.

**Launch geography recommendation:** US App Store storefront only (then US Google Play). Do not list on EU, UK, India or Canada storefronts in v1. Keep the web contribution page reachable worldwide for invitees, because overseas grandparents are a core persona, but collect nothing beyond what the inviting US family needs, run no marketing outside the US, and complete a DPDP readiness review before the DPDP Rules' Data Fiduciary obligations become operational (18 months after notification in November 2025, so roughly May 2027; exact date Unverified) [L11][L12]. Revisit EU/UK only with a full GDPR programme (Art. 9 explicit consent, DPIA, Art. 27 representative, transfer mechanism). Detail in s.5.

## 5. Launch geography recommendation

**Recommendation: United States only for v1 (iOS App Store US storefront; Google Play US later).**

Why:
- The privacy baseline we must build anyway (California, Connecticut, Washington MHMDA, ARL) is achievable for a solo founder. Adding GDPR/UK GDPR means Art. 9 explicit consent for health content, a DPIA, an Art. 27 representative, transfer mechanisms for us-west-1 hosting and every US processor, and EU-specific consumer and accessibility law (EAA). Unverified detail; this is a cost judgement, not a legal conclusion.
- India is strategically important (Hindi, diaspora) but the DPDP Rules' fiduciary obligations become operational around May 2027 [L11], and the regime for children's data (verifiable parental consent for under-18s) needs real design. Selling in India later, after counsel review, is cleaner than launching into a moving rule set.
- The diaspora persona lives mostly in the US (UR s.1.4: Indian Americans), so the US storefront captures the core buyer.

How to handle overseas grandparents (the hard case):
- The parent (US) is the customer and controller-facing user. The web contribution page is a feature of the US family's book, reached only by personal invitation.
- Minimise: name the child by first name only, no account, no email required, no analytics beyond aggregate counts, no cookies beyond the session, audio encrypted in the browser (B-NFR-005).
- Notice: short contributor notice (versioned, see `POLICY_VERSIONING.md`) explaining that the letter goes to the inviting family's book, who can see it, how to delete it via the return link.
- Counsel opinions needed before ~May 2027 (DPDP) and before any EU marketing (GDPR Art. 3(2)).
- Do not geo-block the contribution page in v1: blocking Nani in Jaipur breaks the product's core promise. If counsel disagrees, the fallback is "app-only for overseas contributors" (they install the app from a US-only listing, which is not possible), so a geo-block would effectively remove the feature; escalate to the founder as a product decision.
