# Feature gap and wedge: what else Early Letters must have, and where a second and third reason to choose us could come from

Date: 10 Nov 2026 (UTC). Lane: desk research, published pages only. Agent: user-research. Docs only.
Founder question (10 Nov, verbatim): "We also need to identify what more features this product must have. We can't win only on one feature. So let's think about that."

Nothing here was tested with parents. I did not run any competitor app. Everything about what parents want is a HYPOTHESIS unless a page I read says it.

## 1. Headlines

1. **Voice capture is no longer rare in the baby category.** In one day of looking I found six products that put voice at or near the centre for baby or family memories: Remento's Baby Book of Firsts [P5], Dearest [P6], Tiny Voices [P15], The Days We Keep [P17], Lumelo [P16] and FirstChapter [P14]. The founder is right that one feature will not carry us. Our distinct claim inside voice is narrower: the words stay the parent's, and every machine fix is marked and undoable. Even that is not alone: Remento also offers a cleaned transcript with ums and ahs removed [P4]. What nobody else I read offers is the visible, reversible mark (HYPOTHESIS: I read marketing pages, not the products).
2. **Family sharing is the loudest table stake we do not yet have.** Tinybeans praise centres on private sharing with relatives [P1]. Qeepsake lets a spouse or other contributors share access [P2]. The Days We Keep lets the parent pick the audience per memory [P17]. FirstChapter and Sproutbook lead with family following along [P14][P12]. Our v1.0 is co-parent only, and NX-42 lists outward sharing as "not written" (NX-42 section 3).
3. **A printed book is the category's habit and its money, and its weak point is quality.** One Qeepsake reviewer who prints a book every year also calls the first book disappointing and the collage photos too small [P2]. A words-first book has an opening there (HYPOTHESIS).
4. **Recommendation in brief:** make "the family hears it" (grandparents listen and read, free, no app) the second wedge, and a printed Year One with a QR to the voice the third, because they share one piece of infrastructure (parent-chosen, hosted audio). Build the table stakes first (photo with a letter, find a letter, backup). Languages is a real differentiator but only when the packs pass; treat it as a gate, not a build. Section 7 has the reasons and the founder questions.

What the set tells us:
- **Two clusters.** Photo-first family albums (Tinybeans, Notabli, Chatbooks) and prompt-first journals (Qeepsake, Storyworth, Remento). A third cluster of voice-first baby apps is new and tiny (Tiny Voices, The Days We Keep, Lumelo, FirstChapter all have very few or no ratings on the pages read). That cluster is thin in ratings, not in intent.
- **AI rewriting is spreading** (FirstChapter, Sproutbook, Remento's story mode). It is the one thing our constitution rules out and the clearest contrast we have.
- **Annual price clusters around $35 to $75** among the pages read (Days We Keep $34.99, FirstChapter $39.99, Tiny Voices $49.99, Day One Silver $49.99, 1SE $49.99, Tinybeans listing $74.99). Our $49.99 is mid-pack.

## 5. Candidate second and third wedges

Five to test, not conclusions. Cost sizes are guesses (S under 2 weeks of one engineer, M a few weeks, L a quarter or needs a partner).

### W1. The family hears it (grandparents listen and read, free, no app)

- **User need and evidence.** Relatives want to see and hear the child. Tinybeans' praise is private sharing with relatives who are not on social media [P1]. Remento's storyteller flow works with no app or login, and the company says people over 85 use it [P3][P4]. The Days We Keep gives a per-memory audience [P17]. About 65 million US grandparents, 76% of whom say digital tools are a main way to stay in touch, per our earlier desk research [REPO PRD 02]. Evidence for demand from our own parents: none yet (PRD marks co-parent demand as thin, 1 of 287 reviews [REPO]).
- **Why we could win.** What a grandparent wants to hear is the parent's voice saying what the child did. Photo albums do not carry that; ours does. Free for family removes the Qeepsake-style paywall on contributors [REPO].
- **Cost.** L. Needs audio to leave the phone (today no audio upload), hosted links, access control, a web page (F37 is flagged as "a second product" in the PRD [REPO]), and a privacy-label change.
- **Risk.** A child's voice on our servers changes the promise "on your phone by default" (BRAND). Link leakage. Cost of storage and bandwidth. Support load from elders. Overseas grandparents while the app is US-only (PRD Q3 [REPO]).
- **Constitution check.** Passes if: the parent chooses each letter or circle; shows the words as the parent kept them; no counts, likes or "seen by"; no machine-written caption. A "seen by" receipt is a gap-counting risk; skip it.
- **Cheap test.** Concierge, no build: 8 to 10 parents send one real letter as the existing PDF export plus audio file by their own message to a grandparent. Ask the parent after one week: did the grandparent open it, reply, ask for more? Ask the grandparent one question: could you listen without help? Counts by hand only. Needs consent wording from user-research and nothing logged in the app.

### W2. A printed book that speaks (Year One, words first, QR to the voice)

- **User need and evidence.** Print is the category's habit: one Qeepsake parent has printed a book every year for seven years [P2]; Remento's book carries QR codes to the audio or video [P5]; Chatbooks' whole business is books [P11]; Storyworth includes a hardcover [P7]. The same Qeepsake review calls the first book disappointing and the collages too small [P2]. Price-sensitivity: Qeepsake books called pricey [P2].
- **Why we could win.** Pages of the parent's own words with no tiny collages avoid the quality complaint (HYPOTHESIS). The QR to the original voice is the thing a paper book cannot otherwise hold. It also gives grandparents a reason to buy or ask.
- **Cost.** L. Print partner, layout engine for text, tax and shipping, returns, customer support. A QR needs a hosted link, so W2 depends on the same infrastructure as W1.
- **Risk.** Margin and fulfilment trouble can swamp a small team. Reviews punish print mistakes (the Qeepsake complaint). Brand rule: "Printed product: not promised in v1.0".
- **Constitution check.** The book prints the words as kept, with fixes applied or not as the parent chooses and shown; no layout copy that rewrites; no "you have 14 empty pages" counts.
- **Cheap test.** Make one proof of the PDF export through two print-on-demand services. Hand it to 5 parents and 3 grandparents. Ask what they would pay and what they would change; treat stated price as weak evidence. No in-app promise until the founder rules.

### W3. Every language as spoken, including elders

- **User need and evidence.** Remento supports English and Spanish only [P4]. Nothing I read says Tiny Voices, Days We Keep, Lumelo or Dearest transcribe in other languages (silent, not "no"). Our earlier desk research: 72% of Indian Americans aged 5 and over speak another language at home, and 85% of US Latinos say it matters that future generations speak Spanish [REPO PRD 02]. I did not re-read those sources.
- **Why we could win.** It matches "never translated" and the faithful-edit rule, and the elder who speaks Hindi to {child} is exactly the voice the book should keep. Few rivals appear to try (HYPOTHESIS from silence).
- **Cost.** Per language: device spikes, native-speaker review, rule packs (all draft per LANGUAGES.md [REPO]). Build 12 ships English only (NX-42 C38).
- **Risk.** A promise we cannot keep is worse than none: poor transcription in Hindi would hurt trust. Hindi-English mixing is v1.1 per BRAND. Store listing may name only shipping languages.
- **Constitution check.** Strong fit. Fixes in a language with a draft word table must stay punctuation-safe.
- **Cheap test.** Already in the plan: 10 recordings from 5 multilingual families, scored by a native reviewer on names and word accuracy. If it passes in one language, that language is the wedge; if not, it stays a promise on a roadmap, not in marketing.

### W4. Letters for later (sealed and "open when" letters)

- **User need and evidence.** Dearest and The Days We Keep ship sealed or time-capsule memories [P6][P17]. Parents in our earlier desk research keep an email address for the child and plan to hand it over at 18 [REPO USER_RESEARCH 1.3]; I did not re-read the threads.
- **Why we could win.** The product is literally "letters to {child}". A letter that opens on a birthday is the story we already tell.
- **Cost.** M. A date lock on the phone and a reminder on that day; no server needed for a local version. Server delivery later.
- **Risk.** Durability: a letter must survive 18 years of phone changes, plan lapses and sign-in changes, so it depends on export, backup and sync being right. The brand rule against loss language limits how we describe "if I am not here". Giving a minor access has legal edges (UNVERIFIED; needs counsel).
- **Constitution check.** Fine on words. Watch copy: time, not endings.
- **Cheap test.** Concept cards in the interview round: show three cards (sealed letter, birthday letter, letter at 18) to 8 parents and ask which they would write this month. Count picks by hand. No build.

### W5. Hands-free, one-hand capture (lock screen, Action Button, Siri)

- **User need and evidence.** Parents hold babies on contact naps and want the phone only [REPO USER_RESEARCH 1.1, one anecdote]. Chatbooks sells "one hand" [P11]. Lumelo ships quick-capture widgets [P16]. The Qeepsake praise is about prompts that arrive as texts, i.e. capture at the moment [P2].
- **Why we could win.** Capture speed is our core loop, and crash-safe capture already exists (PRD rank 1). Being first to record in two taps from the lock screen is a daily-use edge.
- **Cost.** M, unknown. Widgets and Siri shortcuts need native iOS extensions; whether Expo SDK 57 handles this cleanly is UNVERIFIED. The widget was already cut from v1.0.
- **Risk.** Recording that starts when the parent did not mean it is a trust failure; App Review will look at mic use from the lock screen; battery.
- **Constitution check.** Fine. Must show clearly that it is recording.
- **Cheap test.** Time-to-first-word on 5 devices with the current build against a lock-screen Shortcut a person sets by hand (Shortcuts app, no code). Then ask 5 parents to use the Shortcut for one week and report.

## 6. What this evidence cannot tell us

- Whether parents choose between these products on any of these features. I found listing text and a few reviews; no purchase-reason data.
- Whether any new entrant in the voice-first cluster has traction. Their rating counts on the pages read were tiny (2 to 10), or none.
- Real price points for sharing and print. Prices here are list prices only.
- Hosting cost for audio. Not estimated.
- Competitor reviews at scale. By rule I did not scrape; the praise and complaint columns are thin.

## 7. Recommendation

1. **Fix the table stakes before chasing a wedge.** Photo with a letter, find a letter and recordings backup are what a parent coming from Qeepsake or Tinybeans will look for in the first week. They are mostly planned; keep them ahead of any new wedge.
2. **Second wedge: the family hears it (W1).** Test with the concierge pilot now (no build). It is the loudest gap and it uses the thing only we hold: the parent's voice beside their unrewritten words.
3. **Third wedge: the printed Year One with a QR to the voice (W2).** Prove it with a hand-made proof before any partner talks. It rides on W1's hosted audio, so do not build one without the other.
4. **Languages (W3) is a gate, not a project.** Name only the languages that pass the device test. If Hindi passes, it is the strongest honest differentiator we have among the pages read.
5. **Park W4 and W5 as cheap tests;** add a sealed-letter row and a quick-capture row to NX-42 so they are not invisible.
6. **Hold the line on traps.** Voice capture is crowded, and the cheapest way to look like everyone else is to add AI recaps, moods or streaks. Our contrast with FirstChapter, Sproutbook and Apple Journal is the constitution.

## 8. Three questions for the founder

1. **Audio leaving the phone.** W1 and W2 need a parent-chosen, hosted copy of a child's recording for grandparents and for the QR. That changes "on your phone by default" and the privacy label. Are you willing, per letter and only when the parent chooses? Recommendation: yes, with the concierge test first and privacy and counsel review before any build.
2. **Print as a business line.** Do you want printed Year One as a revenue line with a partner (margin, shipping, support), or only an export the family prints themselves? Recommendation: hand-made proof first; decide after five parents and three grandparents have held it.
3. **Who is the buyer after the first parent?** Is the grandparent a listener only, an author (F32) or a gift buyer (F43)? The answer decides whether W1 is a feature or a growth channel. Recommendation: listener first, because it needs no new account type.
